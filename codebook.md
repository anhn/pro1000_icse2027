# Codebook: feedback literacy in team reflections (RQ1)

Unit of analysis: a text segment from a team's final-report reflection (or an open survey answer) that refers to feedback or tasks on the feedback platform.

## Inclusion rules
- Include: segments about the platform's feedback, scores, tasks, resubmission, or how the team handled them.
- Exclude from feedback-literacy coding: segments about general AI-tool use (e.g. ChatGPT or Copilot for code or text), about feedback from customers, users or peers, and about course organisation. These were noted as context only.
- A segment expressing two ideas is split, and each part gets the single best-fitting dimension.

## Dimensions (Carless & Boud, 2018) and subthemes
| Dimension | Definition used | Subthemes (inductive) |
|---|---|---|
| Appreciating feedback | Recognising the value of the feedback and the team's active role | Learning support; structure and progress; iteration; conditional appreciation |
| Making judgements | Evaluating the feedback, or own work, against criteria | Specificity and actionability; contextual relevance; consistency with task text; relevance to final deliverable; deciding revision is unnecessary |
| Managing affect | Emotional responses to feedback and how they were handled | Frustration with unclear feedback; uncertainty from inconsistency; ambivalence; time pressure |
| Taking action | Changes made in response to feedback | Direct revision; repeated cycles; conceptual change; downstream project change; process adaptation |

## Collective mechanisms (cross-cutting; Gabelica et al., 2012)
Sharing and distributing; negotiating judgements; coordinating action. Login and submission patterns are coded as *access* only.

## Decision rules and examples
- **Split segment:** GB09's account of its WBS feedback. "Vague, difficult to understand what actually needed to be improved" was coded as *making judgements (specificity)*. "The study advisor should have been more precise" was coded as *managing affect (frustration)*.
- **Contradictory accounts** (praise and criticism of the same feedback) are kept and coded as conditional appreciation or judgement.
- **Negative cases:**
  - Three reports did not mention platform feedback.
  - Four teams completed five or fewer activities.
  - GB01 judged the platform to have great potential for improvement because of technical problems.
